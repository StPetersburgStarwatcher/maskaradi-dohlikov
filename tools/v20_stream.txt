54|<H>Merits and Flaws</H>
63|Merits and Flaws are optional Traits that a Storytell-
54|er may choose to include, or prohibit, in her chronicle. 
54|Properly used, Merits and Flaws help players create and 
54|individualize their characters. Merits are special abili-
54|ties or advantages that are rare or unique in the general 
54|Kindred population, while Flaws are liabilities or disad-
54|vantages that pose challenges to a character’s nightly 
54|existence. These Traits can provide player characters 
54|with added depth and personality, but Storytellers 
54|should be careful to ensure that any Traits chosen will 
54|not adversely influence the course of the chronicle or 
54|give one character an unfair advantage over the rest.
63|Merits and Flaws can be selected only during char-
53|acter creation and are purchased using freebie points 
53|(although existing Merits and Flaws can be removed 
53|or new ones added by the Storyteller during the course 
53|of the chronicle). Each Merit has its own point cost, 
53|while each Flaw has a point value which adds to the 
53|amount of freebie points a player can spend during the 
53|creation process. A character may take as many Mer-
53|its as the player can afford, but no character may have 
53|more than seven points’ worth of Flaws (which would

316|Merits and Flaws are divided into four categories: 
307|physical, mental, social and supernatural. The physi-
307|cal category describes Merits or Flaws that deal with 
307|a character’s physical makeup or abilities, while the 
307|mental category addresses intellectual abilities or pat-
307|terns of behavior. The social category comprises rela-
307|tionships and status in Kindred or mortal society, while 
307|supernatural Merits and Flaws concern the paranormal 
307|abilities of vampires and the way they interact with the 
307|physical world. Storytellers are encouraged to not only 
307|restrict and disallow any Merits and Flaws that don’t fit 
307|with their chronicles or for specific characters, but also 
307|to create their own to reflect unique elements of their 
307|games. Some example restrictions are given in the text 
307|of each Merit and Flaw, but Storytellers can allow (or 
307|disallow) any combination that don’t make sense for 
307|their games.

307|hearing, smell, touch, or taste. The difficulties for all

54|tasks involving the use of this particular sense are re-

54|<E>Ambidextrous (1pt. Merit)</E>
63|You have a high degree of off-hand dexterity and 
54|can perform tasks with the “wrong” hand at no pen-
54|alty. The rules for taking multiple actions still apply, 
54|but you do not suffer a difficulty penalty if you use two

54|<E>Bruiser (1pt. Merit)</E>
63|Your appearance is sufficiently thug-like to inspire 
54|fear (or at least disquiet) in those who see you. While 
54|you’re not necessarily ugly, you do radiate a quiet men-
54|ace, to the point where people cross the street to avoid 
54|passing near you. All Intimidation rolls against those 
54|who have not demonstrated their physical superiority 
54|to you are at -1 difficulty.
54|<E>Catlike Balance (1pt. Merit)</E>
63|You possess an innately perfect sense of balance. 
54|Characters with this Merit reduce difficulties of all bal-
54|ance-related rolls (e.g., Dexterity + Athletics to walk 
54|along a narrow ledge) by two.
54|<E>Early Riser (1pt. Merit)</E>
63|No one can explain it, but you seem to have the abil-
53|ity to work on less rest than your fellow vampires. You 
53|always seem to be the first to rise and the last to go to 
53|bed even if you’re been out until dawn. Your Human-
53|ity or Path score is considered to be 10 for purposes of 
53|deciding when you rise each evening. Vampires with 
53|this Merit cannot take the Deep Sleeper Flaw.
54|<E>Eat Food (1pt. Merit)</E>
63|You have the capacity to eat food and even savor its 
54|taste. While you cannot derive any nourishment from 
54|eating regular foods, this ability will serve you well in 
54|pretending to be human. Of course, you can’t digest 
54|what you eat, and there will be some point during the 
54|evening when you have to heave it back up.
54|<E>Friendly Face (1pt. Merit)</E>
63|You have a face that reminds everyone of someone, 
54|to the point where strangers are inclined to be well-
54|inclined toward you because of it. The effect doesn’t 
54|fade even if you explain the “mistake,” leaving you at 
54|-1 difficulty on all appropriate Social-based rolls (yes 
307|for Seduction, no for Intimidation, for example) when 
307|a stranger is involved. This Merit only functions on a 
307|first meeting.
307|<E>Blush of Health (2pt. Merit)</E>
316|You look more hale and healthy in appearance than 
307|other vampires, allowing you to blend with human so-
307|ciety much more easily. You still retain the color of a 
307|living mortal, and your skin feels only slightly cool to 
307|the touch.
307|<E>Enchanting Voice (2pt. Merit)</E>
316|There is something about your voice that others 
307|cannot ignore. When you command, they are cowed. 
307|When you seduce, they swoon. Whether thundering, 
307|soothing, persuading, or simply talking, your voice com-
307|mands attention. The difficulties of all rolls involving

307|<E>Daredevil (3pt. Merit)</E>
316|You are good at taking risks, and even better at sur-
307|viving them. When attempting exceptionally risky 
307|non-combat actions (such as leaping from one moving 
307|car to another), characters with this Merit add an ad-
307|ditional three dice to their rolls, and negate a single 
307|botch die that may result from such a roll. Generally, 
307|such actions must be at least difficulty 8 and have the 
307|potential to inflict at least three health levels of dam-
307|age if failed.
307|<E>Efficient Digestion (3pt. Merit)</E>
316|You are able to draw more than the usual amount 
307|of nourishment from blood. When feeding, you gain 
307|an additional point to your blood pool for every two 
307|points of blood you consume. This does not allow you 
307|to exceed your blood pool maximum.
307|<E>Huge Size (4pt. Merit)</E>
316|You are abnormally large in size, at least 6’10” and 
307|300 pounds in weight (well over two meters tall and 
307|over 130 kgs). Aside from making you extremely no-
307|ticeable in public, this extra mass bestows an addition-
307|al Bruised health level. Characters with this Merit may 
307|also gain bonuses to push objects, open barred doors, 
307|avoid being knocked down, etc.
307|<E>Hard of Hearing (1pt. Flaw)</E>
316|Your hearing is defective. The difficulties of any rolls 
307|involving the use of hearing are increased by two.

54|your identity. Examples include a nervous cough, con-
54|stantly wringing your hands, cracking your knuckles, 
54|and so on. It costs one Willpower to refrain from en-

54|<E>Bad Sight (1 or 3pt. Flaw)</E>
63|Your sight is defective. The difficulties of any die 
54|rolls involving the use of your eyesight are increased by 
54|two. As a one-point Flaw, this condition can be cor-
54|rected with glasses or contacts; as a three-point Flaw, 
54|the condition is too severe to be corrected.
54|<E>Fourteenth Generation (2pt. Flaw)</E>
63|You were created five or fewer years ago by a mem-
53|ber of the Thirteenth Generation. Though you have 
53|10 blood points in your body, only eight of them may 
53|be used to heal wounds, power Disciplines, raise Attri-
53|butes, etc. You can still use the final two blood points 
53|for other purposes, though. The blood point costs of 
53|nightly rising, creating and sustaining ghouls, and cre-
53|ating blood bonds remains the same as for other vam-
53|pires. You cannot raise any Discipline above four dots.
62|Taking this Flaw precludes you from taking the Gen-
53|eration Background, and you may not start with Status, 
53|either. You are likely a Clanless Caitiff, for your blood 
53|is probably too thin to pass down the distinguishing 
53|characteristics of a Clan. Most Fourteenth-Genera-
53|tion vampires should also take the Thin Blood Flaw 
53|(p. 483).
54|<E>Fifteenth Generation (4pt. Flaw)</E>

54|points can be used for Disciplines, healing or raising

307|blood points to obtain the effect a normal vampire

307|not create or sustain ghouls, create a blood bond, or 
307|sire a vampiric childe. You can use the remaining four 
307|blood points to survive through the day and wake up

316|You cannot raise any Discipline above three dots.
316|The weakening of the Curse of Caine has compen-
307|sations, though (which distinguish this Flaw from the

54|<H>Ghouls</H>
63|A vampire’s blood has great power — power that 
54|can be passed on. Long ago, the Kindred discovered 
54|that mortals who drank vampiric blood would become 
54|possessed of supernatural abilities and dark appetites. 
54|Soon it became common practice for vampires to keep 
54|certain favored servants, fattening them on vitae, and 
54|blood bonding them in the process. In doing so, they 
54|created undying, hellishly loyal servitors — ghouls.
63|Most ghouls exist at the whim of their domitors (mas-
54|ters), serving loyally in exchange for vitae. As they are 
54|as susceptible to the blood bond as anyone, ghouls are 
54|almost without exception unfailingly devoted to their 
54|masters — and if the domitor has more than one ghoul 
54|servant, the result may be a jealous struggle between 
54|them for the domitor’s favor. A ghoul’s emotions run to 
54|extremes with the heady drug of vitae in his veins — such 
54|a creature often falls prey to great rages and disturbing 
54|cravings. The name “ghouls” was not chosen at random.
54|<H>Types of Ghouls</H>
54|<E>Vassals</E>
63|<I>“No, you listen to me. I don’t care who you are or who </I>
54|<I>you’re with; the lady asked not to be disturbed. And she </I>
54|<I>isn’t going to be. Capisce?”</I>
63|When most vampires think of ghouls, they picture 
54|the vassal. Many Cainites find it inconceivable to cre-
54|ate ghouls for any other purpose; indeed, to most Kin-
54|dred, any ghouls other than vassals are blasphemies. 
54|By and large, vassals are the “normal people” among 
54|ghouls. Most of them didn’t ask for their new power or 
54|immortality, although few complain too much once the 
54|blood bond takes effect. Scant few of them know even 
54|a quarter what a neonate does about Kindred society. 
54|Somewhere along the line, they were just singled out 
54|by something old and powerful, something that wanted 
54|another pair of hands. Although a vassal’s supply of vi-
54|tae is pretty much assured, that’s really all she has to 
54|look forward to. In fact, she’d better be on her very 
54|best behavior if she wants to keep her liquid strength 
54|coming regularly. She also has to come to terms with 
54|the fact that she’s in love — truly, passionately, in a 
54|way she didn’t think she’d ever feel again after her first 
54|love left — with a person who treats her with passing 
54|kindness at best and draconian cruelty at worst.
63|The sad thing is that the great majority of vassals 
54|really have no idea what’s going on. They don’t know 
307|why they feel the way they do; they don’t know what’s 
307|in that miracle drink their new friend gives them. But 
307|they’re loyal, if for no other reason than they don’t 
307|want the magic to stop. They don’t want to go back to 
307|the monochromatic, tasteless lives they knew before. 
307|And you know what? Those are the lucky ones. Be-
307|cause once a ghoul’s been dragged to the other side of 
307|that mystery, once the Masquerade’s been broken, she 
307|exists entirely at the sufferance of her domitor.
307|<E>Independents</E>
316|<I>“You know just us well as I do that you can’t do </I>
307|<I>everything yourself, or have you forgotten that you’re </I>
307|<I>exclusively nocturnal? Trust me, I’m reliable help, and my </I>
307|<I>price isn’t hard to pay at all.”</I>
316|You think being a junkie is rough? Try doing it when 
307|the dealers want to shoot you on sight. An indepen-
307|dent‘s road is a hard one. Kindred view these masterless 
307|ghouls as dangerous and uncontrollable, and a vampire 
307|very much mistrusts what he doesn’t control. Usually, 
307|the only recourse is to kidnap and kill vampires in or-
307|der to gain their blood. Others serve vampires as free-
307|lance “help,” taking care of particularly nasty daylight 
307|problems.
316|Independent ghouls come from all places, but they 
307|put a bit of distance between themselves and ordinary 
307|people. They aren’t pawns; they do what they do delib-
307|erately. Usually, they’re orphans: A domitor dies, and 
307|his enemies are careless enough to let a servant or two 
307|get away. Sometimes the orphan’s been a ghoul for so 
307|long that he knows he’ll wither away to old age in a 
307|month if he doesn’t get some vitae. Desperation gets a 
307|lot of independents started down this road.
316|Of course, some independents have more compli-
307|cated stories. Some may have stumbled across Cain-
307|ite society accidentally, and eventually discovered the

307|even have been cannibalistic killers who picked an un-
307|usually strong victim, and learned the source of that
