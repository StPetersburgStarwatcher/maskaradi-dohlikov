96|<H2>Enemies</H2>
96|On the other side of Allies are the background Flaw 
96|Enemies. If your Allies run a small private detective 
96|agency, their Enemies could be yours: a gang of 
96|smash-and-grab thieves who prey on their clients 
96|(and your Domain), or a police department using the 
96|bureaucracy to endlessly demand useless paperwork 
96|or seize evidence intended for you. You may also just 
96|not have Allies at all, only Enemies who try and throw 
96|as many impediments into your path as possible.
105|However, you can try using this Flaw to your 
96|advantage: manipulate the Enemies by using one of 
96|your Masks to deniably send them after your own 
96|foes, or frame them for such an attack on a rival. Of 
96|course, Enemies already have plenty of motivation 
96|to trace such mischievousness back to you or your 
96|coterie – and you run the risk of letting them meet 
96|your Kindred foes. Discuss such tactics with the 
96|Storyteller, and with any other players in the group, 
96|as this Flaw can affect them as well. Just like Allies, 
96|Enemies are always mortals, or in some cases, ghouls.
96|<H>Contacts</H>
96|Where Allies help you make things happen, Contacts 
96|help you learn about things happening to others. 
96|How you have access to a particular Contact does not 
96|have to synergize with your background or Concept 
96|(a resourceful Kindred has eyes and ears everywhere 
96|after all) but a concrete reason for this specific Contact 
96|makes the story better and more plausible. Street-level 
96|coteries can leverage one and two-dot Contacts who 
96|provide street-level items and information as part of 
96|your shared milieu.
105|Go ahead and put a name and basic personality 
96|to a Contact, on the Relationship Map or in 
96|your notes. A character who worked as a radio 
96|personality may know the tech crew from their 
327|studio, or a mortician may still have a connection to 
327|their funeral home. How your death changed your 
327|relationship with them – or if it didn’t change it at 
327|all – provides juicy roleplaying potential in even a 
327|quick “drug buy” scene. As the vampire ages and 
327|meets new mortals to add to their arsenal, playing 
327|the game and being opportunistic about mortals 
327|you meet can create new Contacts and open up new 
327|possible story and resource opportunities.
327|<H>Fame</H>
327|Taking Fame as a background generally means that 
327|the Storyteller characters in a chronicle want to have 
327|access to you. There are two ways that a vampire may 
327|have Fame in a modern setting, and each carries their 
327|own dangers. Mortal fame, and having to be in the 
327|public eye, puts these Kindred at risk of being sniffed 
327|out by the Inquisition, or of being blackmailed by 
327|unscrupulous fellow Kindred. Choosing to have 
327|Fame among Kindred reduces that chance, but means 
327|that members of other sects or factions may see you as 
327|a potential ally, or threat, or source of information or 
327|access to even more famous Cainites. The Storyteller 
327|may wish to craft a Loresheet around a character’s 
327|Fame, or offer the player the option of taking their 
327|Fame from an existing Loresheet group or legend.
336|Having Fame can make it difficult to get around 
327|places discreetly, what with humans tailing you 
327|wishing to post selfies with you, but it can protect 
327|you in some ways. Kindred risk breaching the 
327|Masquerade by overtly harming or attacking a 
327|Famous character, with human entourages and 
327|paparazzi always on the lookout in the area. You can 
327|also escape enemies by ducking into exclusive clubs 
327|or hotels with year-long waiting lists.

301|<H>Herd</H>
301|Some Predator types rely on this Background more 
301|heavily than others, such as Consensualist, while the 
301|Predator type Blood Leech won’t be able to make 
301|effective use of this Background at all. The chronicle 
301|may also help decide how Herds are used, if allowed 
301|at all, as contested territories, domains in war zones, 
301|or nomadic and fugitive coteries make it harder to 
301|access this Background. Herds may also blend into 
301|other Backgrounds, allowing you to get more story 
301|mileage out of mortals. Retainers and Allies may 
301|have tie-ins to a player’s Herd, and the Storyteller 
301|may allow players to use similar combinations with 
301|other Backgrounds as well.

301|Based in political power,<B> </B>Influence adds intrigue and

301|more than just manipulating city politics or media 
301|in the abstract. Vampires of certain clans may have

96|easier times adding and using Influence in various

96|<H2>Despised and Disliked</H2>
96|These two Flaws allow for interesting gameplay if you 
96|wish to crank up your vampires’ self-reliance in their 
96|own Domain. Despised turns one group or region 
96|of the city actively against you, and even neutrals

96|rallying your own neighborhood. Of course, when 
96|you send your Allies and Retainers into other hoods,

96|<H>Loresheets</H>
96|Loresheets exist to highlight a certain aspect of 
96|the world for the interested player and Storyteller, 
327|without forcing every player to master the entire 
327|minutiae of the <B>Vampire</B> setting. A Loresheet 
327|foregrounds some group, historical event, or elder 
327|<I>as it relates specifically to the player,</I> encouraging 
327|them to take ownership of that aspect and 
327|reminding the Storyteller that vampiric arcana 
327|exists to serve the story at the table tonight, not as 
327|mandatory history lessons.

327|acts as its own Background type.

327|You have been Embraced into a distinct bloodline

327|<B>Masquerade,</B> p. 190) apply — “Bloodline” is a

301|powerful Adversaries target you as leverage against

301|nominal superior.

301|advancement within your sect ever since. Or reverse

301|great enemy. Perhaps they betrayed you, framed

301|court or exiled. Now you’re back, and your greatest 
301|goal is to bring your Adversary down.

327|they exist in a world hostile to them, and where they

327|Kindred society, with doors rarely opening toward sect

327|the scales against them. The powers that be rarely allow 
327|thin-bloods to feed in prime locations without facing

327|desirable piece of territory more powerful vampires chase 
327|them out of it. Some thin-bloods seclude themselves in 
327|neighborhoods nobody wants, or even drift to smaller 
327|towns like the Caitiff did in the last centuries.

327|from more traditional organizations. Instead, they
